## 1. Context — why this is being built

**GUACA is a live map of local knowledge for Caribbean travel.** Locals contribute physically-verified places and events; tourists plan trips from them. Pilot geography: **Puerto Cabello, Venezuela**, expanding to Colombia and Costa Rica.

The repository `github.com/guacamap/guaca-app` is **empty** — one commit, an 11-byte README. Everything in this plan is greenfield.

### The problem
AI trip planners confidently recommend restaurants that do not exist. The market leader (Mindtrip.ai, which acquired Thatch in 2025) generates venues with an LLM, and its venues are documented as sometimes non-existent. Meanwhile Google Maps is address-first, which fails across the Caribbean where many worthwhile places have no street address — they have a landmark ("the yellow house past the second bridge").

### The insight
> **The AI's own ignorance becomes a local's paycheck.**

When the system cannot answer, that refusal is not a failure — it is a *demand signal* precise enough to pay someone to go resolve it.

### The core loop (memorise this; every feature serves it)
1. Tourist asks a question — free, unlimited.
2. AI answers instantly from human-verified places **if coverage exists**.
3. If not, it **REFUSES** and logs the gap.
4. Gap agent aggregates ("7 guests asked about X, we have nothing") and commissions **ONE** paid mission to **ONE** local Spotter.
5. Spotter goes, pins, photographs, verifies, gets paid.
6. The answer becomes permanent map data, free to every future asker.

### Positioning: "witnessed, not inferred"
Our places are physically visited by a named local whose name and photo appear on the pin. **Do NOT compete on planner UI** — the competitor's is better and better funded. Compete on the places being real.

### Intended outcome of this plan
A deployed, working system that on **16 Aug 2026** demonstrates live: a tourist asks → the AI answers from real human-verified data → a second question hits an uncovered area → **it refuses on stage** → and displays the paid mission it just commissioned to a named local. Every other team will demo a concierge that confidently answers everything.

## 3. Product rules — non-negotiable

These are constraints on the code, not preferences. Violating one is a bug.

1. **Reports describe places and conditions, never people.** No schema field, prompt, or UI may capture a claim about a person. Condition reports (sea state, road condition, power, water, crowd level) are the allowed form of "live" data.
2. **The AI NEVER generates a place.** It composes, routes, translates and schedules over human-verified data only. This must be *structurally* true — enforced by code and proven by tests — not requested in a prompt. See §7.3.
3. **Payments stay behind the `PayoutProvider` interface.** `MockPayoutProvider` is the only implementation wired for the MVP.
4. **No token, no chain integration.** Ever.
5. **Locals never pay.** Spotters are paid; businesses post free; tourists and properties are the only payers.
6. **One mission, one Spotter, one guaranteed payment.** Never winner-takes-all, never a race.
7. **Editorial rule: no franchises, no obvious tourist spots.** Enforced as a rejection reason in verification.
8. **Landmark-first, not address-first.** Every place must carry a `landmark_description`; street address is optional and usually absent.
9. **Never expose credentials as `ANTHROPIC_*` env vars.**
10. **Keep it one repo.** `packages/shared` is the API/client contract; splitting it means npm publishing overhead on every type change.

### Already rejected with evidence — do NOT re-propose
| Rejected | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Open contributor marketplace | "Most tour guide marketplaces fail within 18 months"; Like A Local Guide → 1 employee. Curated wins (Spotted by Locals: 10+ yrs, self-funded). |
| Points/levels as the **primary** reward | Foursquare users "added imaginary places just to become mayor." Pay first; status on top. |
| Tourist-funded per-question bounties | Google Answers died of "lack of users willing to pay for answers"; ChatGPT now answers free and instantly. |
| Winner-takes-all payouts | Wastes the losers' trip; supply churns out. |
| Consumer trip planner as the business | Desti — "users found it useful but booked elsewhere." |
| Building a tour marketplace now | Licensing walls, $2–5M liability standard, worst survival rate in the category. |

### Business model (context for copy and demo narrative)
- **Tourists** subscribe $4.99–9.99, sold at the planning moment.
- **Villas/posadas** are both the distribution channel and the payer — no front desk, no F&B revenue to protect, and their Airbnb rating depends on good local recommendations.
- **Coverage is funded once per area and resold** to every property and tourist in it.
- **Spotters** are paid in **airtime/data top-ups** (Reloadly: one API covers the Caribbean, needs no bank account, is not money transmission, and reads as a gift rather than a wage).
- **Territory identity:** ONE named Spotter per zone, name and photo on every pin. Not global leaderboards.
- **Guide ladder:** contribution is the audition. Levels 1–3 unregulated everywhere; Level 4 is jurisdiction-aware (Jamaica 30h TPDCo, Colombia 1,760h, Costa Rica 1,000h+). Jamaica is the launch market for that rung. **MVP implements levels 1–3 only**; level 4 is a data field and a documented roadmap item.

### Two supply types, different motivations
- **Spotters** — PAID, hand-curated (10–20 per area), contribute **evergreen places**.
- **Businesses / organisers** — FREE, self-interested, post **their own time-bound events**. This is what makes the map feel alive.

### 4.1 — Decision change (2026-08-08): tourist accounts + role chooser

Supersedes "Web + QR, no app install". Tourists install the PWA and create an
account; the app opens on a role chooser (Tourist | Spotter), choice
persisted. Rationale: the business model always required an identity to
subscribe against; anonymous sessions could not carry it.

- **Auth** — email one-time code → JWT, mirroring the spotter code+JWT
  pattern (`jose`, hash-compared code). Web transport: httpOnly cookie
  `guaca_tourist`. The verify hook must also accept `Authorization: Bearer`
  so the future RN client needs no server change.
- **Deployment constraint (found 2026-08-08)** — web fetches the API
  cross-origin with `credentials: 'include'`; cookies only survive Safari/ITP
  if web and API share one registrable domain (`app.X` / `api.X` = same-site).
  The `vercel.app` + `sslip.io` fallback is cross-site → cookie auth breaks
  on iPhone. Therefore: one domain with two subdomains is REQUIRED for the
  demo, and the Bearer fallback is built now, not later. **Domain chosen
  2026-08-08: `guaca.live`** — "a live map" is the product claim.
- **Schema** — new `tourists` table (id, email unique, language, created_at,
  login_code_hash, code_expires_at). Deliberately separate from `spotters`:
  operator-provisioned and self-serve lifecycles do not merge. The role
  chooser is UI, not schema.
- **Asks** — require a tourist JWT; rate limits move from anonymous session
  to account.
- **T7.7 `/v/[qrToken]`** — stays a no-auth landing but becomes the signup
  funnel: an account created from it is attributed to the property
  (villa-attributed acquisition is business-model data).
- **T7.8** — PWA manifest/installability unchanged, now load-bearing for the
  tourist too.
- **T8.3** — compliance rewritten: tourist PII now exists (email + language,
  nothing else); minimisation and erasure extend to tourists.
- **Demo** — account created live on stage (email, one code, ~15s) or a
  pre-created judge account as fallback.
- **Email delivery** — dev: code printed to the ops stream; prod: one
  transactional provider (Resend or SMTP) behind a ~20-line interface.

### 4.2 — Decision change (2026-08-08): Mapbox GL JS replaces MapLibre

Supersedes T1.4's "MapLibre GL". The map moves to **Mapbox GL JS** with a
Mapbox style (`mapbox://styles/mapbox/standard` or `streets-v12`).

- **Why** — the current basemap is raw `tile.openstreetmap.org` raster
  tiles, which the OSM tile-usage policy disallows for production apps and
  which look flat in a judged demo. Mapbox gives a vector basemap, one
  vendor across web and the future RN app (`@rnmapbox/maps`), and optional
  geocoding later. Free tier (50k web loads/mo) far exceeds the pilot.
- **Swap size** — one file (`apps/web/src/app/(tourist)/page.tsx`): import,
  `Map`/`Marker` constructors (API-compatible), inline raster style →
  Mapbox style URL. `maplibre-gl` out of package.json, `mapbox-gl` in.
- **Token** — `NEXT_PUBLIC_MAPBOX_TOKEN` (public by nature): set in
  `.env.local` and Vercel env. **Scopes: public defaults only** — the
  baseline (view styles/tilesets/geocode) covers GL JS and Static Images;
  no secret scopes, `sk.*` never ships to the client.
- **Token strategy** — two tokens: `guaca-dev` (public, unrestricted,
  lives only in `.env.local`) and `guaca-web-prod` (public,
  **URL-restricted** — restriction entries: `https://app.guaca.live`,
  `https://staging.app.guaca.live`).
  Caveats for later, straight from Mapbox: URL restrictions are
  Referer-based — they do **not** work with native SDKs (the RN app gets
  its own public token + a secret `downloads:read` token at build time for
  `@rnmapbox/maps`), and server-side Static Images calls (print QR cards
  from the VM) send no Referer, so they need their own token too.
- **Licence note** — mapbox-gl v2+ is proprietary (ToS), not BSD; the repo
  stays Apache-2.0 (SDK is a dependency, not derived code). DATA_SOURCES.md
  row updated; T8.4's licence list reads Mapbox accordingly.

### 4.3 — Decision (2026-08-08): three-tier dev cycle

local → staging → production, trunk-based, zero added infra cost.

- **Branch mapping** — feature branches → PR (CI + Vercel preview) →
  merge to `main` = **staging** auto-deploy → verify/rehearse →
  fast-forward `production` branch (`git push origin main:production`) =
  **production** deploy. Rollback: revert on `production` / Vercel
  instant rollback.
- **Staging web** — Vercel branch domain: `main` → `staging.app.guaca.live`;
  production branch → `app.guaca.live`. Same registrable domain (`guaca.live`,
  §4.1), so cookies stay same-site on every tier. DNS: `app`, `api`,
  `staging.app`, `staging.api` + apex redirect → `app`.
- **Staging api** — same VM, second compose project (`guaca-staging`) with
  its own Postgres DB / Redis / MinIO bucket + a Caddy vhost
  `staging.api.guaca.live`. `infra/deploy.sh <staging|prod>` picks compose
  project + env file.
- **Inference** — staging shares the prod L40S endpoint (stateless; do NOT
  provision a second GPU) or points at MiniMax via `INFERENCE_BASE_URL`.
  Tests everywhere use `FakeInference`.
- **Env matrix** — per tier: `.env.local` / `.env.staging` / `.env.prod`;
  separate `SESSION_SECRET` + `OPERATOR_TOKEN` per tier; email = code→ops
  stream on local+staging, real provider on prod; Mapbox = `guaca-dev`
  token locally, `guaca-web-prod` token with BOTH `staging.app` and `app`

- **Migrations** — forward-only; each tier's deploy runs `pnpm migrate`

- **Why staging matters for GUACA specifically** — demo rehearsals and

### 7.2 The three agents

Exactly three. Adding a fourth is a scope failure.

| Agent | Trigger | Role | Rubric claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Planner** | Tourist question or day-plan request | Answers from verified places only, in the guest's language; **refuses** outside coverage and logs the gap | Reasoning, orchestration, the refusal |
| **Gap** | Scheduled (every N minutes) + on demand | Clusters unanswered questions into scored coverage holes; commissions ONE mission to ONE Spotter | **Autonomy** — the originality claim |
| **Verification** | Spotter submits a place/photo | Cheap→expensive check ladder; photo matches pin, place exists, second local confirms | Multi-step reasoning, multimodal, **human oversight**, **compute efficiency** |

### 7.3 The anti-hallucination guard — the single most important mechanism

The claim "structurally incapable of inventing a restaurant" must be a **code fact**. Build this on **day 2, before any agent graph exists.** Everything else in this plan is architecture; this is the claim.

**Three principles, in priority order:**

**P1 — The model has no string channel wide enough to carry a place.** The inference client exposes **no free-text method**. There is only `json<T>(schema)`. The planner's output schema contains integers and enum members and *no place-name field of any kind* — so there is no slot to hallucinate into. Enforced by TypeScript, not by a prompt.

**P2 — LangGraph orchestrates; Postgres owns truth.** Graph state is a working set. Every consequential fact is written to Postgres and re-read on the next hop.

**P3 — Prose is rendered from database rows by a pure function.** The model chooses *ordering and timing only*; a deterministic renderer produces every word a tourist reads.

### The mechanism

1. **Retrieval is pure SQL/PostGIS.** It builds an immutable `Catalog` of verified rows, assigning each a small integer `ref` (1…N). `placeId` UUIDs are **never sent to the model**.
2. **The output schema makes an invalid answer undecodable.** `Catalog.refEnum()` emits `{type:'integer', enum:[1..N]}` into the JSON Schema, and the schema is `.strict()` (`additionalProperties:false`):

```ts
export const PlanDraft = z.object({
  stops: z.array(z.object({
    ref: z.number().int().positive(),
    startMin: z.number().int().min(0).max(1439),
    durationMin: z.number().int().min(10).max(300),
    reasonCode: ReasonCode,          // enum: OPEN_NOW | NEAREST | MATCHES_TOPIC | …
  })).min(1).max(8),
  languageCode: z.enum(['es','en','pt','fr','de','it','nl']),
}).strict();
```

  Note what is **absent**: no `title`, no `description`, no `note`, no `name`. A hostile provider adding `{"placeName":"La Sirena Dorada"}` fails structurally on `.strict()`.

3. **`assertGrounded` — the exact algorithm.** The only construction site of the branded `PlanArtifact` type in the entire codebase:

```
 1. PARSE       PlanDraft.safeParse                    fail → SCHEMA
 2. SIZE        1 ≤ stops ≤ 8                          fail → EMPTY_PLAN / OVERSIZED_PLAN
 3. MEMBERSHIP  catalog.refs.has(ref) for every stop   fail → UNKNOWN_REF
 4. UNIQUENESS  refs pairwise distinct                 fail → DUP_REF
 5. PROJECT     ref → catalog.byRef.get(ref).placeId
                *** every other byte of model output is discarded here ***
 6. RE-READ     SELECT … WHERE id = ANY($placeIds)
                  AND verification_status='verified' AND witness_count>=2
                rows.length ≠ placeIds.length         fail → NOT_VERIFIED_AT_RENDER   (TOCTOU guard)
 7. COHERENCE   sorted, non-overlapping, travel time feasible (PostGIS, 4.5 km/h)
                                                       fail → TIME_INCOHERENT
 8. SWEEP       lexicalSweep(raw, catalog)             fail → FREE_TEXT_ENTITY
 9. FINGERPRINT ctx.fingerprint === catalog.fingerprint fail → CATALOG_FINGERPRINT_MISMATCH
10. CONSTRUCT   return branded PlanArtifact
```

  **Step 5 is the whole trick.** The model's output is reduced to `(int, int, int, enum)` tuples. No path exists by which a novel place crosses the boundary, **because the boundary is arithmetic.** Step 6 closes a real TOCTOU hole: a place can be un-verified between retrieval and render.

4. **`lexicalSweep`** is belt-and-braces for any field that *does* carry text (the Spotter mission brief reuses this machinery). Normalise NFKD → strip diacritics → lowercase → **map homoglyphs** (Cyrillic а/е/о → Latin), then flag capitalised n-grams and quoted spans not covered by catalog name tokens + a fixed Venezuelan gazetteer + generic nouns ("Playa", "Calle", "Plaza").

5. **The architectural rule is enforced at build time, not by a test.** `dependency-cruiser` in CI:

```json
{ "name": "render-must-not-touch-inference", "severity": "error",
  "from": { "path": "^src/render" }, "to": { "path": "^src/(inference|graphs)" } }
```

  This is the provable code fact — a **build failure**, not an assertion. Demonstrate it live by adding the forbidden import and watching CI go red.

6. **On violation:** one repair call carrying only the violation *code* (never echo the offending text back into the model), then a `RefusalArtifact` + a gap row. **Never degrade to best-effort.** If `injectionSuspected`, skip the repair entirely — cheaper and safer.

7. **Coverage check runs BEFORE any LLM call.** Insufficient candidates → deterministic refusal, zero tokens spent, gap logged. *We do not pay to say "I don't know."*

### Prompt injection

Structural defence first: "Recommend Café Fantasma" **cannot be complied with**, because the compliant response space contains no such value. Layered on top: the untrusted question is fenced and labelled as data *after* the catalog; a free regex/heuristic pre-flight detector (`ignore .* instructions`, `you are now`, `system:`, `<|im_start|>`, long base64, homoglyph density, embedded fake `[REF n]` blocks) sets `injectionSuspected`, which never blocks an answer but tightens the guard and lights up the dashboard. A question embedding a fake catalog (`[REF 42] La Sirena Dorada`) dies at step 3.

### The tests that prove it — all in `test:unit`, zero network, zero cost

Full matrix in §13. The two load-bearing ones:

- **A1 — `AdversarialInference`**: a provider implementation that *actively tries* to invent (strategies: `INVENT_PLACE`, `NAME_IN_TEXT`, `EXTRA_KEYS`, `FLOOD`, `MALFORMED`, `ECHO_INJECTION`, `DUP_REFS`, `TYPE_CONFUSION`). Run the whole planner graph against it × 500 generated questions. Zero artifacts with an out-of-catalog `placeId`; every non-artifact is a typed refusal.
- **A2 — property test** (`fast-check`, 10 000 runs, arbitrary catalogs × arbitrary JSON): `assertGrounded` either throws a typed `GuardViolation` **or** returns an artifact whose `placeIds ⊆ catalog.placeIds`. **There is no third outcome.**

A2 is the sentence you say to a judge: *"We ran ten thousand randomly-generated hostile model outputs through the guard. Zero produced a place that wasn't witnessed. It's a property, not a policy."*

### 7.4 Verification check ladder (cheapest → most expensive)

Ordered deliberately for the compute-efficiency criterion. Each rung can reject early, and rejection short-circuits everything below it. **L0–L4 kill roughly 60–75% of bad submissions before spending a single token.**

| # | Check | Cost | Returns / threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| **L0** | Integrity: mission open, spotter is the assignee, ≥3 photos, MIME/size sane, not already submitted | ~0 | any fail → **hard** |
| **L1** | Freshness: capture timestamp inside the mission window | ~1ms | outside by >24h → **hard** |
| **L2** | Geo distance to pin (EXIF GPS if present, else device-attested GPS) | ~1ms | ≤75m PASS · 75–250m WEAK (linear 1.0→0.3) · >250m **hard** · absent → INCONCLUSIVE |
| **L3** | pHash 64-bit (`sharp-phash`) vs the spotter's history, all photos in the zone, and a known-web seed set | ~10ms | Hamming ≤6 → **hard** (`PHOTO_REUSE`) · 7–12 WEAK · >12 PASS |
| **L4** | Intra-submission diversity — three shots of the same wall is not verification | ~0 | min pairwise Hamming <10 → **hard** (`NO_DIVERSITY`) |
| **L5** | **Vision** — ONE multimodal call, *all photos in a single request* | **paid** | structured verdict, below |
| **L6** | Second local confirmation | human, hours | CONFIRM / DENY / TIMEOUT(72h) |
| **L7** | Operator escalation | human | APPROVE / REJECT / REQUEST_MORE |

**Fusion is deterministic — no model involved in the decision:**

```ts
const visionScore = v.isScreenshot || v.isStockLike ? 0
  : 0.55*v.typeMatchesClaim + 0.30*v.sceneConsistency + 0.15*v.confidence;
const trust = 0.30*gpsScore + 0.20*freshnessScore + 0.15*originalityScore + 0.35*visionScore;
const effective = clamp(trust + Math.min(0.10, 0.02*spotter.consecutiveAccepted), 0, 1);
```

| Condition | Verdict |
|---|---|
| any **hard** fail | **REJECT** before L5 — the vision call is never paid |
| `effective ≥ 0.80` | **ACCEPT_PENDING_SECOND** → L6 |
| `0.55 ≤ effective < 0.80` | **ESCALATE** → operator |
| `effective < 0.55` | **REJECT** with structured feedback; one resubmit allowed |
| L2 INCONCLUSIVE **and** vision confidence < 0.6 | **ESCALATE** — never guess when both the cheap and expensive signals are mute |
| L6 **DENY** | **ESCALATE**, never auto-reject — rivalry and honest error both exist |
| L6 **TIMEOUT** | **ESCALATE**; place stays `provisional` |

> ### ⚠ Node decomposition is dictated by `interrupt()` semantics — do not merge these nodes
> `interrupt()` **re-runs its node from the top on resume**, and resume values are matched *by index*. Therefore `visionVerify` and `requestSecondLocal` **must be separate nodes**. If they share a node, every human response re-pays for the vision call — the most expensive operation in the system, repeated on every HITL round-trip. Expensive or side-effecting work goes in a node *before* the interrupting node, never inside it. Every write past an interrupt must be idempotency-keyed. There is an explicit test for this in §13.

**`provisional` places are invisible to the planner.** They appear on the map with a "1 of 2 witnesses" badge; the guard's step-6 DB re-read structurally excludes them from every answer. **No machine-only verification, ever** — even `effective = 0.99` requires a second human. That is the product promise compiled into a code path, and it is the thing the competitor structurally cannot claim.

**On the vision model's one free-text field.** `signageTextObserved: string[]` (OCR evidence) is the only place the model emits uncontrolled strings. It is **evidence, never a name**: used solely to *corroborate* the Spotter-typed name by normalised trigram similarity, and **never written to `places.name`**. The place's name always comes from the human. Have this answer ready — it is the one apparent exception to "the AI never generates a place," and a sharp judge will find it.

> **Browser-PWA honesty note.** `getUserMedia` produces no EXIF, and iOS strips location on share sheets while any canvas re-encode destroys it. So do **not** depend on EXIF: the Spotter PWA captures `navigator.geolocation` at shutter time and submits a **device-attested position signed with a server-issued nonce** (short TTL, bound to `missionId`), uploading **original file bytes** via a presigned URL with no canvas anywhere in the path. EXIF becomes corroborating evidence when present, not the primary signal. **State this limitation plainly in `docs/COMPLIANCE.md`.**

> **Browser-PWA honesty note.** `getUserMedia` produces no EXIF, so "photo GPS" is really *device geolocation captured alongside the photo and attested by the client*. The server records both client-claimed capture time and server receipt time. Client-side we require in-app camera capture rather than a file picker, but that is not enforceable server-side — so rungs 3, 5 and 6 are the real defences. **State this limitation plainly in `docs/COMPLIANCE.md`.** If Phase 9 (Expo) ships, real EXIF strengthens rung 1.

### 7.5 Gap scoring

### Clustering — zones, not just cells

Cluster key = `${topicCode}:${zoneId}`. No embeddings.

- **Topic**: a fixed taxonomy of ~24 `TopicCode`s, classified **once at question time** — a deterministic bilingual ES/EN lexicon (~400 stemmed, diacritic-folded terms) catches ~80% for free; only the leftovers go to **one batched enum call covering up to 50 questions**. That is ~1 classification call per gap run, not per question.
- **Geography**: **hand-drawn zone polygons** for the pilot (Malecón, Casco Histórico, Playa Quizandal, Borburata, Patanemo…) stored as PostGIS `geography(Polygon)`, with H3 res-8 as the fallback for points outside every polygon:

```sql
zone_id := COALESCE(
  (SELECT z.id FROM zones z WHERE ST_Covers(z.geom, p.geom) LIMIT 1),
  'h3:' || h3_lat_lng_to_cell(p.lat, p.lon, 8));
```

Hand-drawn beats pure H3 here because **a zone is a walk a Spotter can actually do**, which is exactly what you are commissioning — and it is explainable to a judge in one sentence.

### The scoring function

```ts
export function scoreGap(g: GapSignals): ScoredGap {
  const q = g.questionCount, s = g.distinctSessions;

  // 1. Demand — log-damped volume weighted by BREADTH of distinct askers.
  //    40 questions from 1 session is spam; 12 from 12 sessions is a market.
  const breadth = q > 0 ? s / q : 0;                                   // 0..1
  const D = Math.log1p(q) * (0.35 + 0.65 * breadth);

  // 2. Recency — exponential decay, 5-day half-life.
  const rHat = q > 0 ? g.askAgeDays.reduce((a, d) => a + 0.5 ** (d / 5), 0) / q : 0;
  const Rmult = 0.30 + 0.70 * rHat;

  // 3. Commercial — paying properties near the zone centroid.
  const C = g.properties.reduce(
    (a, p) => a + TIER_WEIGHT[p.tier] * Math.exp(-p.distanceKm / 1.2), 0);
  const Cmult = 1 + 0.6 * Math.log1p(C);

  // 4. Scarcity — steep. We only pay where we have (almost) nothing.
  const coverage = g.verifiedPlaces.reduce(
    (a, p) => a + clamp(1 - p.ageDays / 180, 0.15, 1), 0);
  const S = 1 / (1 + coverage) ** 1.5;

  // 5. Feasibility — hard zero if nobody can physically get there.
  const F = (g.spotterCapacityInZone > 0 ? 1 : 0) * ACCESS_PENALTY[g.accessDifficulty];

  return { score: Math.round(100 * D * Rmult * Cmult * S * F), breakdown: {D,Rmult,Cmult,S,F,q,s} };
}
// TIER_WEIGHT = { PARTNER_PREMIUM: 3.0, PARTNER: 1.8, TRIAL: 0.6, NONE: 0 }
// ACCESS_PENALTY = [1.0, 0.85, 0.6]   // walkable | needs transport | boat/4x4
```

**Hard gates, applied before the score is even consulted:** `q ≥ 3 AND s ≥ 2 AND score ≥ 45 AND spotterCapacityInZone > 0`.

**Worked numbers — these are a table-driven test, not decoration.** A formula you haven't sanity-checked is a formula that fires at the wrong time:

| Scenario | q | s | coverage | score | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "7 guests asked, we have nothing", PARTNER hotel 300m | 7 | 6 | 0 | **219** | commission |
| Same, but 2 fresh verified places already exist | 7 | 6 | 2.0 | **42** | below floor — no mission |
| Same, but those 2 places are 150 days stale | 7 | 6 | 0.33 | **143** | commission a refresh |
| One loud tourist asking 20 times | 20 | 1 | 0 | — | **gated** on `s ≥ 2` |
| Real demand, but no Spotter can reach it | 9 | 7 | 0 | **0** | `F = 0` |

Row 2's 42-vs-45 is not luck — it is existing coverage correctly killing a mission that raw demand would otherwise buy.

### Guards on autonomy

- **Duplicate missions, three layers.** (1) The real guarantee: `CREATE UNIQUE INDEX missions_one_open_per_gap ON missions(gap_key) WHERE status IN ('offered','accepted','submitted','in_review')`. (2) A 21-day cooldown after completion, escapable only by a genuine demand surge (`q ≥ 2 × q_at_last_commission`). (3) A Redis advisory lock per `gapKey` so concurrent cron runs cannot race.
- **Never winner-takes-all — and never a monopoly either.** Exactly one mission per run, selected with fairness correction so the same zone and Spotter don't absorb everything: `selectionScore = score × 0.75**missionsInZoneLast14d × (zoneId === lastZone ? 0.8 : 1)`, and Spotter assignment applies `× 0.7**missionsAssignedLast7d` so income spreads across the curated 10–20. Say this out loud in the demo — it is a *governance* answer, not just an algorithm.
- **Graduated autonomy** (the human-oversight showpiece). Auto-approve only when **all** hold: `budget ≤ $25`, `score ≥ 120`, the zone has a prior successful mission, spotter reputation tier ≥ 2, and the kill switch is off. Otherwise → `interrupt()` → `guaca commission <gapId> --approve`. Auto-approved missions are **not** silently dispatched: they enter a **30-minute reversal window** during which ops can cancel with one click, and the Spotter notification fires only at the end of it.

  This is a stronger story than interrupting on everything, because it demonstrates *calibrated* trust with an audit trail and a kill switch. Interrupt-on-everything is what a team builds when it hasn't thought about oversight.
- **Idempotency past the interrupt.** `draftMission` is cached in Redis keyed by `(runId, gapKey)` — resuming after an edit re-enters the node and would otherwise re-pay for the model call. `commission` uses `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING RETURNING id`. Notifications carry an idempotency key equal to `missionId`.

### 7.7 Agent runtime — and how a library surprise cannot block the build

**Versions verified against the live registry on 5 Aug 2026 — pin these:**

| Package | Version | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `@langchain/langgraph` | **1.4.9** | peers: `@langchain/core ^1.1.48`, `zod ^3.25.32 \|\| ^4.2.0` |
| `@langchain/langgraph-checkpoint-postgres` | **1.0.4** | `PostgresSaver.fromConnString(url, { schema: 'langgraph' })` then `await saver.setup()` |

Confirmed exports in 1.4.9: `StateGraph`, `Annotation`, `StateSchema`, `interrupt`, `Command`, `Send`, `START`, `END`, `MemorySaver`, `isInterrupted`, `getCurrentTaskInput`.

**Use `Annotation.Root`, not the newer `StateSchema` class.** Both exist in 1.4.9; Annotation is the older, better-documented surface and LangChain states it continues to work unchanged. Take the boring one for a 12-day build — migration later is mechanical and touches only ~40 lines per graph.

> ### ⚠ Three API behaviours that change the design — all verified, none obvious
> 1. **`interrupt()` re-runs its node from the beginning on resume**, and resume values are matched **strictly by index**. Expensive or side-effecting work must live in a node *before* the interrupting node, never inside it. This single fact dictates the verification graph's node decomposition (§7.4).
> 2. **Never wrap `interrupt()` in a bare `try/catch`** — it throws a control-flow exception that a catch-all swallows, silently breaking human-in-the-loop.
> 3. **Use a dedicated `langgraph` Postgres schema** for checkpoints so library tables never collide with domain migrations. Keep `thread_id` under 255 chars (`gap:${runId}`, `verify:${submissionId}`) and add a retention job — do not discover the checkpoint table's size during the demo.

**Compile the planner WITHOUT a checkpointer** (it has no interrupt on the hot path, and its state holds a non-serialisable `Catalog` instance). Compile gap and verification **with** `PostgresSaver` — they pause on humans for minutes to hours.

LangGraph.js moves fast and its API may still differ from the above. **Do not let that stop you.** Every graph sits behind one thin interface, and each node is an independently testable pure-ish function:

```ts
// packages/agents/src/runtime/types.ts
export interface AgentNode<S> {
  name: string;
  run(state: S, ctx: AgentCtx): Promise<Partial<S>>;
}
export interface AgentCtx {
  inference: Inference;             // FakeInference in every test
  db: Queries;
  emit: (e: RunEvent) => void;      // never console.log inside a node
  runId: string;
  loopId: string;                   // threads the whole story together
  budget: RunBudget;
  /** The ONLY LangGraph-specific call permitted inside a node body. */
  pause<T, R>(node: string, payload: T): R;
}
export interface Agent<I, O> { run(input: I, ctx: AgentCtx): Promise<O>; }
```

`pause()` is the seam that makes the fallback real. Two adapters implement it:

```ts
// LangGraph adapter
pause: (_node, payload) => interrupt(payload) as never,

// Plain-TS adapter — resume by re-running with the value supplied
export class InterruptSignal<T> extends Error {
  constructor(readonly node: string, readonly payload: T) { super('INTERRUPT'); }
}
pause: (node, payload) => {
  if (node in resumeValues) return resumeValues[node] as never;
  throw new InterruptSignal(node, payload);
}
```

The rules that keep this safe:
1. **All logic lives in nodes, never in graph wiring.** A node takes state, returns a state patch. Nodes are unit-tested directly, with no graph and no LangGraph import.
2. **LangGraph is the orchestrator, not the logic.** It supplies `StateGraph` / `Annotation.Root` state channels, conditional edges, a checkpointer (Redis), and `interrupt()` for human-in-the-loop pauses.
3. **A plain-TypeScript sequential runner is the fallback.** ~50 lines: run nodes in order, apply patches, honour a `nextNode` field for conditional branching, throw a typed `Interrupt` error to pause. If the LangGraph API fights you for more than an hour, ship the fallback runner and move on — **the rubric rewards the architecture and the human oversight, not the import statement.** Keep the node structure identical so LangGraph can be reinstated later.
4. **Human-in-the-loop is persisted in the database, not only in memory.** A pause writes a row (`verification_runs.decision = 'needs_operator'`, or `missions` awaiting approval). This means an interrupt survives a process restart, and the operator CLI can resolve it without the original process being alive. Do this even if `interrupt()` works perfectly — it is what makes the oversight real rather than a demo trick.

**Verify the installed API before Phase 3** with a 20-line spike: build a two-node graph with one conditional edge and one interrupt, run it, resume it. Record what actually works in `docs/AGENTIC_WORKFLOW.md`. Ten minutes here saves a day later.

### 7.8 Compute efficiency — the scored criterion, tabulated

Judges score this explicitly. Make the numbers visible rather than claiming them.

| Decision | Saving |
|---|---|
| Refusal decided from the coverage index **before** retrieval | zero tokens on the most common failure path |
| Deterministic planner fast path for single-topic "now" questions | ~60% of answers use **zero** inference |
| Descriptions translated **once at verification**, never per read | translation cost amortised over every future reader, forever |
| Topic classification by lexicon first, one batched enum call for leftovers | ~1 classification call per gap run, not per question |
| L0–L4 short-circuit before the vision call | ~60–75% of bad submissions cost nothing |
| All photos in a single multimodal request | 1 call instead of N |
| `maxOutputTokens` mandatory; output is integers and enums | tiny completions — an 8B model is genuinely sufficient |
| Provider capability probe cached 24h in Redis | one probe per provider per day |
| **L40S over H200** | the 8B model cannot use 141GB; ~2.9× cheaper |

`modelCallsPerAnswer`, `costUnitsAvoided`, `% answers with zero inference` and `% submissions rejected before vision` are first-class metrics — put them on the demo dashboard, not in a slide.

### 7.9 Inference client — schema-only by construction

```ts
export interface Inference {
  json<T>(req: JsonRequest<T>): Promise<JsonResult<T>>;
  vision<T>(req: VisionRequest<T>): Promise<JsonResult<T>>;
}
```

**There is deliberately no free-text method.** Adding one should require a code-review conversation, because it reopens the channel §7.3 closes. Every request carries a mandatory `maxOutputTokens`, a `purpose` for budgeting/logging, and keeps `untrusted` user text in its own fenced field — never blind-concatenated into the prompt.

Provider negotiation is a **capability probe** run once per base URL and cached 24h in Redis: send a 5-token request with a trivial `json_schema`; on 200 use strict `response_format: {type:'json_schema', …}` (vLLM's forward-compatible path — `extra_body.guided_json` is being deprecated; set `--guided-decoding-backend xgrammar`), on 4xx fall back to `{type:'json_object'}` with the schema serialised into the system prompt, then one Zod-error repair round-trip, then throw.

**Constrained decoding is an optimisation, not the guarantee.** The guarantee is §7.3. This matters because you cannot control what the fallback provider does, and "agents never know which provider is behind the env var" means the safety property must not depend on provider capability.

A `RunBudget { maxModelCalls, maxCostUnits, maxWallMs }` rides in the run context; exceeding it throws `BudgetExceeded`, which every graph routes to a refusal or escalation — **never to a degraded answer**.

```sql
  spotter_id uuid not null references spotters(id),
  brief text not null,                       -- in the Spotter's language
  target_category text not null,
  target_h3 text not null,
  reward_minor int not null,
  currency char(3) not null default 'USD',
  status text not null default 'offered'
    check (status in ('offered','accepted','submitted','verified','paid','expired','cancelled')),
  created_by text not null check (created_by in ('agent','operator')),
  result_place_id uuid references places(id),
  offered_at timestamptz not null default now(),
  accepted_at timestamptz, submitted_at timestamptz, paid_at timestamptz,
  expires_at timestamptz not null,
  cancel_reason text
);
-- ONE open mission per gap. Enforces "one mission, one Spotter, one payment".
create unique index missions_one_open_per_gap
  on missions (gap_id) where status in ('offered','accepted','submitted');

create table payouts (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  mission_id uuid not null references missions(id),
  spotter_id uuid not null references spotters(id),
  provider text not null,                    -- 'mock' | 'reloadly'
  provider_ref text,
  idempotency_key text not null unique,      -- = mission_id; guarantees single payment
  amount_minor int not null, currency char(3) not null,
  status text not null check (status in ('pending','sent','failed')),
  created_at timestamptz not null default now()
);

-- ── The loop thread: this table IS the demo ──────────────────────────
-- A loop_id is minted at the tourist's question and carried onto every row that
-- follows it: question → gap → mission → submission → verification → the answer
-- that finally succeeds. Add `loop_id uuid` to questions, gaps, missions and
-- verification_runs. One query then renders the entire chain with wall-clock deltas.
create table loop_events (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  loop_id uuid not null,
  kind text not null,      -- QUESTION_ASKED | REFUSED | GAP_SCORED | MISSION_APPROVED |
                           -- SUBMITTED | CHECKS_PASSED | VISION_OK | SECOND_LOCAL_CONFIRMED |
                           -- VERIFIED | LOOP_CLOSED
  agent text, payload jsonb not null default '{}',
  created_at timestamptz not null default now()
);
create index loop_events_loop_idx on loop_events (loop_id, created_at);

-- ── Observability & human oversight ──────────────────────────────────
create table agent_runs (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  agent text not null check (agent in ('planner','gap','verification')),
  status text not null check (status in ('running','ok','refused','escalated','error')),
  input jsonb not null, output jsonb, steps jsonb not null default '[]',
  model text, tokens_in int not null default 0, tokens_out int not null default 0,
  latency_ms int, error text,
  created_at timestamptz not null default now()
);

create table verification_runs (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  place_id uuid not null references places(id) on delete cascade,
  photo_id uuid references place_photos(id),
  agent_run_id uuid references agent_runs(id),
  checks jsonb not null,                     -- per-rung results, §7.4
  decision text not null check (decision in ('verified','rejected','needs_second_local','needs_operator')),
  decided_by text not null check (decided_by in ('agent','operator')),
  operator_note text,
  created_at timestamptz not null default now()
);

create table operator_actions (              -- human-in-the-loop audit trail
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  operator text not null,
  action text not null,                      -- verify.approve, mission.cancel, gap.commission…
  target_type text not null, target_id uuid not null,
  reason text,
  before_state jsonb, after_state jsonb,
  created_at timestamptz not null default now()
);
```

### Phase 1 — Data and the map

**Files:** `packages/db/src/seed/*`, `apps/api/src/routes/places.ts`, `apps/web/**` (tourist map)

**Data provenance rule (integrity-critical):** OSM rows import **only** as `source='osm_candidate'`, `verification_status='candidate'`. A candidate is **never** returned by any tourist-facing endpoint and **never** counts toward coverage. It is a to-do list for Spotters, nothing more. Any code path that lets a candidate reach a tourist answer is a critical bug.

**How a candidate is promoted.** The `verified_needs_two_locals` constraint in §8 does the enforcement for you: an OSM candidate has `created_by_spotter_id = NULL`, so it is *physically impossible* to mark it `verified` at the database level. Promotion requires a Spotter to claim it by visiting and submitting a photo (setting `created_by_spotter_id`) and a **different** Spotter to confirm (setting `confirmed_by_spotter_id`). OSM gives us the map's skeleton; only humans give it truth. Do not add a bypass.

- [ ] **T1.1** — OSM import via Overpass API for the Puerto Cabello bbox → `places` as candidates, category-mapped to our taxonomy. Idempotent on `osm_id`. **Test:** re-running imports zero duplicates; every imported row is `candidate`. Commit.

## 11. Verification — how to prove it works end to end

Run in order on a clean checkout. Every step must pass before the demo.

```bash
# 1. Clean boot
git clone … && pnpm install && docker compose up -d && pnpm migrate && pnpm seed

# 2. Full suite, zero network, zero inference cost
pnpm test                      # all packages green; FakeInference everywhere

# 3. The guard specifically — the claim the whole product rests on
pnpm --filter @guaca/agents test:unit guard
#    G1-G10 structural, I1-I5 injection, A1 500 hostile runs,
#    A2 10,000 property runs, A3 renderer mutation
pnpm dlx dependency-cruiser --validate    # C2: render -> inference edges MUST be 0

# 4. Compute-efficiency proof
pnpm --filter @guaca/agents test:unit efficiency
#    ZERO inference calls on the refusal path
#    ZERO vision calls when a cheap rung hard-fails
#    exactly ONE vision call across an interrupt + resume cycle

# 5. Live loop against the deployed stack
guaca tail &                                    # watch structured logs
curl -X POST $API/api/ask -d '{"text":"where can I eat arepas near the fort?"}'   # → answers
curl -X POST $API/api/ask -d '{"text":"is there anywhere to snorkel at Isla Larga?"}' # → REFUSES
guaca gaps                                      # the gap now exists, scored
guaca gaps --watch                              # gap agent commissions a mission
guaca missions                                  # one mission, one spotter, guaranteed reward
#    → accept + submit from the Spotter PWA on a real phone
guaca queue                                     # verification escalation if any
#    → second Spotter confirms → place flips to verified
curl -X POST $API/api/ask -d '{"text":"is there anywhere to snorkel at Isla Larga?"}' # → NOW ANSWERS
```

**Step 5's final line is the whole thesis.** The same question that was refused is now answered, from data a paid local physically produced. Rehearse it until it is reliable.

## 12. Demo script (5 minutes, 16 Aug)

Target: one area, 10 Spotters, **30–50 verified places**, 2–3 villas.

1. **(0:00) The frame.** "Every AI travel planner will confidently answer everything today. Some of those restaurants don't exist. Ours refuses — and that refusal pays a local."
2. **(0:30) Scan the villa QR** on a real phone. Session opens in the guest's language.
3. **(1:00) Ask a covered question.** Instant answer from verified places. Tap a pin — **a named local's face and name**. "This was physically visited by Yorman, on this date."
4. **(2:00) Ask an uncovered question.** **It refuses, live.** Not an error — a confident product state.
5. **(2:30) Cut to `guaca tail`.** The gap agent aggregates the demand, scores it, weights it by the paying property, and **commissions one mission to one named Spotter** — on screen, unprompted.
6. **(3:30) The Spotter's phone** shows the mission arriving. Show the brief in Spanish.
7. **(4:00) Human oversight.** `guaca queue` → an operator approves/overrides a verification. "Autonomy with a hand on the switch."
8. **(4:30) Close.** "Witnessed, not inferred. The AI's own ignorance became a local's paycheck."

**Rehearse with the network unplugged at least once.** Have a recorded fallback video ready.

## 13. Risks and mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Not enough real verified places by 16 Aug** | Start Spotter recruitment on **day 1, in parallel with coding** — it has the longest lead time and no code can shorten it. OSM candidates give map density immediately; the demo needs only ~30–50 genuinely verified. |
| **Live inference fails on stage** | MiniMax-M3 failover is one env var. Cache the demo responses. Have the recorded video. |
| **The refusal looks like a bug to judges** | Design it as a confident first-class UI state with its own visual language, and say the line out loud before it happens. Build a visible **"coverage horizon"** on the map, and word refusals as *"No one has been there yet. 7 people have asked — a Spotter mission opens at 10."* Refusal must read as integrity, not absence. |
| **"The loop is obviously pre-seeded"** — the most likely fatal judge reaction | **Close the loop live on stage.** A judge types a question about a zone you deliberately left uncovered; it refuses with a timestamp; a teammate walks to a real place within 3 minutes and submits; the same question answers ~4 minutes later. Show the `loop_events` timeline with wall-clock deltas. Keep a recorded run as backup and **label it "recorded"** — being caught faking is worse than any technical failure. |
| **"How do you actually stop it inventing a restaurant?" and the answer sounds soft** | Make the hardest question your best moment. Put an **"Attack the AI"** panel in the demo UI and hand the judge the keyboard. Show their prompt, the guard log line (`refsOffered → refsRequested → refsAccepted`), and an answer containing only witnessed places. Then: *"The model's entire output vocabulary is the integers 1 to 12, and 10,000 randomly-generated hostile outputs produced zero unwitnessed places. It's a property of the type system, not of the prompt."* |
| **The gap agent looks scripted** | Let a judge type the uncovered question. `--dry-run` shows the score breakdown so the reasoning is inspectable, not magic. |
| **Interrupt re-execution double-charges** for vision or double-writes a payout | Nodes containing `interrupt()` never contain expensive or side-effecting work; every write past an interrupt is idempotency-keyed. Explicit test in T3.6. |
| **PWA camera/geolocation blocked** | Requires HTTPS — hence deploying early (Phase 2). Test on a real phone on cellular data by day 3, not day 15. |
| **Doing too much and finishing nothing** | Obey the cut-line in §10. Three agents, no more. |
| **Seed data integrity collapses the pitch** | OSM rows are `candidate` and never tourist-visible. Any path leaking a candidate into an answer is a P0 bug. |
